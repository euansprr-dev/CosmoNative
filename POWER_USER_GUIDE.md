# Cosmo Power User Guide

*The operating manual for the system you built. July 2026.*

---

## 1. The One Mental Model: The Cosmo Loop

Everything in Cosmo is a stage in a single loop. Every surface exists to move material one stage to the right:

```
CAPTURE  →  TRIAGE  →  STUDY  →  CRYSTALLIZE  →  DEVELOP  →  SHIP  →  LEARN
 (⌥C,        (Inbox      (Inquiry,   (Seedbed →      (Idea       (Content   (Calendar
  Telegram,   Atlas)      Swipe       Concept         bench)      pipeline,   scoreboard,
  iOS,                    Study,      pages)                      Calendar)   patterns)
  swipes)                 Deep Dive)
```

The confusion of "what do I do in here?" almost always resolves to one question: **what stage is this material in, and which room moves it forward?** Raw captures don't belong on a canvas — they belong in the Inbox. A half-understood source doesn't belong in an idea — it belongs in a Study session. A ripe concept doesn't belong in your head — it belongs on a concept page. An idea with a working hook doesn't belong on the bench anymore — it belongs in the Content pipeline.

The second law of the system: **things accrue, they are not filed.** The Atlas routes captures toward the places they can do work. Seedlings accrue mass across sessions before they earn a page. Concepts mature section by section. Ideas ripen before promotion. You never make an "organize everything" decision — you make one small decision at the moment of capture or triage, and the system compounds them.

## 2. The Map — every room and what it's for

### Capture layer (get it in, zero friction)

- **⌥C Capture Anywhere** — system-wide glass panel from any app, any Space, even full-screen. Non-activating: Esc returns you exactly where you were. Accepts typed thoughts *and drags* — files from Mail, Safari images, screenshot thumbnails. This is the single most important habit in the system.
- **Capture lanes + slash commands** — teach the capture panel your own verbs (Inbox → Manage Commands). A lane pins a destination so repeated captures skip triage entirely.
- **Telegram bridge** — capture from your phone into the same pipe: text, links, voice notes (auto-transcribed via Whisper), topic aliases to route straight to a destination, even remote writing sessions.
- **iOS app** — Inbox tab, orb-fan creation, swipe saves with cloud media mirror. Phone captures defer hard routing questions to the Mac — the phone is for *catching*, the Mac is for *deciding*.
- **Paste a link** — Instagram/YouTube/X links become swipes; the Railway worker fetches media + transcripts and runs the full insight pass in the cloud before you even open them.

### Triage layer

- **Inbox** — one calm ledger, keyboard-clearable end-to-end. The **Atlas router** reads each capture and proposes destinations across *every* kind of home: thinkspaces, clusters, concept pages, deep dives, **open inquiry questions** (a capture can advance a question!), clients, and the Seedbed. It abstains honestly to Unsorted instead of guessing. Your job in the Inbox is verbs, not folders: *merge, place, advance, seed, discard*.

### Organization layer

- **Thinkspaces** — spatial canvases, saved as atoms, nestable under projects. Furniture: **clusters** (named zones that the router can target), **places** (⌘D-saved camera positions), **portals** (windows into other thinkspaces), **live query blocks** (auto-updating searches), **idea boards** (live client idea lists), **templates**, and **flows** (drawn cluster→output automations).
- **Three lenses on one thinkspace**: **Canvas** (spatial thinking), **Library** (Finder-grade browser — icons/list/gallery, marquee select, Space to peek, Enter to rename — for housekeeping at scale), and **Deep Dive** (the topic-mastery home attached one-to-one to the thinkspace).
- **Command Center** — the operational home: Things-3-style task lists with natural-language capture ("Write at 6pm every Tue"), habits, focus timers, weekly reports, and the **Content Calendar**.

### Synthesis layer (the heart of the app)

- **Inquiry Sessions (the Study)** — one manuscript page with a trail, a reading panel (web pages, YouTube via transcript reader, scans), and a capture dock. Everything you capture mid-session is **routed live** to the question or concept it advances, with correctable receipts. You end a session with the **Debrief teach-back** — explaining what you learned is the exit ritual, not a summary screen.
- **Deep Dive Overview** — the topic's home: root questions, open questions, lexicon, seedlings, session archive, and a map. This is where you see a topic's *shape* — what's answered, what's growing, what's stalled.
- **Concept Seedbed** — concepts accrue mass while you research and **never become pages on their own**. Ripeness is legible (≥4 captures across ≥2 sessions, a heavy single-session run, or a pin). Ripe seedlings go through the **Concept Desk** — a development conversation — to earn a page.
- **Swipe Study** — the bench for one saved swipe: analysis rail (hook type + score + mechanism, slide-anchored structure, narrative, voice markers), serif hero, media stage, transcript. Its one tinted button is **Use in Idea** — the bridge from study to creation.
- **Research focus mode** — infinite canvas with a transcript spine and anchored annotations, for when a source deserves spatial dissection rather than linear reading.

### Crystallization layer

- **Concept pages (Connection workspace)** — 3-pane: navigator | board / outline / manuscript | inspector, with a maturity crystal. Concepts mature *section by section*; the inline assistant stages edits as ghost rows on the board so organizing is a review, not a rewrite. This is your permanent, compounding knowledge base — the thing all the studying produces.
- **Ask Your Cortex** — `?` prefix in ⌘K. Answers synthesized *from your own atoms* with numbered citations that open the source, and it says "I don't know" when your cortex genuinely doesn't. This is both retrieval and a self-test: if Cortex can't answer it, you haven't crystallized it.

### Creation layer

- **Ideas** — a first-class destination: working set in motion, one shelf per client. Each card is a hook in serif with its inspired-by swipe.
- **Idea bench** — the development room: hooks lab (working-hook star, AI ghost suggestions, ⌥↑/⌥↓ reorder), framework beats on the outline, ripening control, and overlays to **link swipes** (structural DNA) and **link concepts** (substance).
- **Content focus** — the manuscript room: 7-phase pipeline (ideation → draft → polish visible), brainstorm view, AI collaborator, marginalia rail, ship sheet.
- **Content Calendar (Command Center → Upcoming)** — the month is the planning surface. The **Shelf rail holds what still needs a decision; the calendar holds what's decided; ideas are seeds and never consumed.** Drag from Shelf onto a day to commit. Scheduled posts sit as client-colored chips.

### Learning layer

- **Past days on the calendar are the scoreboard** — published posts land on their real ship date with real numbers once performance is logged.
- **Pattern engine** — every swipe's insight pass produces a signature card; the cross-swipe PatternWeaver and the Discover feed's outlier detection surface *what's working in your niches* continuously.
- **Automation rules** — built-in hygiene rules ship disabled (auto-enrich ideas, stale-content nudge, spark auto-advance, cluster auto-link, Telegram routing). Turn them on once you trust the loop.

### Cross-cutting: ⌘K and the inline assistant

- **⌘K Cortex** is the connective tissue: lexical-first search across everything, quick-add composer (full NLP task capture), calculator, Readwise library, swipe gallery, `@kind` scoped queries, `?` Ask Cortex — and **drag-out retrieve**: drag any result out of ⌘K and drop it onto the canvas.
- **The inline assistant** is one system that follows you into every writing surface (notes, ideas, content, concepts, thinkspace copilot) with skills (`/review`, `/riff`, `/concept`, Organize Workspace) and diff-based, never-blocking apply. You don't go to the AI; it's ambient in whatever room you're in.
- **Panes & windows** — split view runs panes as tabs (up to 3); any atom can open in its own window; Space peeks nearly everywhere; the navigation trail island gets you back from anywhere.

## 3. The Playbooks

### Playbook 1 — The Content Engine
*Swipe → Study → Idea → Draft → Calendar → Scoreboard. The bread-and-butter loop.*

1. **Stock the pond.** Import creators (Swipe → Creators); Discover becomes an editorial feed of outliers in your pillars. Save anything with a mechanism worth stealing — every save gets the full cloud insight pass automatically. All swipes are curated by definition; don't second-guess saves.
2. **Study on a rhythm, not on demand.** Work the *New This Week* shelf in Swipe Study 2–3 times a week. Read the analysis rail against the actual slides: what's the hook mechanism, what's the structural recipe. You're building taste, not archiving.
3. **Bridge immediately.** The moment a swipe sparks something, hit **Use in Idea** — the idea is born already linked to its structural inspiration. Never let a spark live only in your head.
4. **Develop on the bench.** In the Idea bench: hammer hooks in the hook lab (star the working one), let the framework beats shape the outline, link the concept pages that give the piece substance. An idea with a starred hook + linked swipe + linked concept is *loaded* — writing it is transcription.
5. **Write in Content focus.** Promote when the idea is ripe; move through ideation → draft → polish; `/review` and `/riff` in the margins.
6. **Commit on the calendar.** Drag the draft from the Shelf onto a day. The Shelf is your decision queue — keep it short.
7. **Close the loop.** Log performance; past days become the scoreboard; winners inform which mechanisms you study next. This is the flywheel — most people run steps 1–6 and skip 7, which is why they plateau.

### Playbook 2 — The Scholar's Loop
*Question → Study sessions → Seedlings → Concept pages → Cortex. How knowledge actually compounds here.*

1. **Give the topic a home.** A thinkspace + its Deep Dive. Write **root questions** first — the Deep Dive is question-driven, and everything downstream routes toward questions.
2. **Study in sessions, never ambiently.** Launch an Inquiry Session from the Deep Dive (or any question). Load the source in the reading panel and capture *as you go* — claims, questions, quotes. The dock routes each extract live to the question it advances; correct misroutes from the receipt capsules. You never take notes "to file later."
3. **Debrief every session.** The teach-back is where understanding gets tested. Skipping it is the difference between reading and studying.
4. **Let the Seedbed do its job.** Don't force concepts into pages. Recurring ideas accrue mass; the ripeness badge tells you *why* something is ripe. Meanwhile, inbox captures that smell like insights also feed the Seedbed — your shower thoughts and your reading converge on the same seedlings.
5. **Develop ripe seedlings at the Concept Desk.** The development conversation is deliberate: a concept earns its page by surviving your articulation of it.
6. **Mature pages section by section.** In the Connection workspace, work the board; let the assistant stage organizational edits as ghost rows and review them. The maturity crystal shows you what's still thin.
7. **Test with `?`.** Ask Cortex questions you *should* be able to answer. Citations open the sources; "no knowledge" answers are your gap map. When new captures can *advance a question*, even your inbox becomes part of studying.

### Playbook 3 — Frictionless Ops
*Capture everything, decide once, execute from one room.*

1. **One hotkey everywhere.** ⌥C on the Mac, Telegram on the phone, iOS inbox on the go. The bar for capturing must be zero — trust the Atlas to sort it later.
2. **Triage as a session, not a drip.** Open the Inbox once or twice a day and clear it from the keyboard. Accept good routes fast; the router abstains rather than lies, so Unsorted is honest and small. Teach it with lanes and slash commands for your recurring capture shapes.
3. **Tasks live in Command Center only.** Smart capture parses times and recurrence; day-pinned tasks, habits with time goals, focus timers. Use `@mentions` in task capture to link tasks to the atoms they serve.
4. **Run the day from the dashboard**, the month from Upcoming, and read the weekly report to recalibrate.

### Playbook 4 — The Studio (spatial thinking)
*When a project deserves a room, not a list.*

1. One thinkspace per real project. Name **clusters** with intent — the router reads cluster charters, so a well-named cluster is a self-filing folder.
2. Save **places** (⌘D) for the regions you return to; drop a **portal** to the related thinkspace instead of duplicating material.
3. Put **live query blocks** and **idea boards** on the canvas — the workspace updates itself while you're away.
4. When a canvas gets crowded, switch to the **Library lens** and do Finder-grade housekeeping (marquee select, batch move, peek). Or ask the thinkspace copilot to **Organize Workspace** and review its clustering.
5. Draw **flows** for the cluster→output behaviors you repeat, and enable the built-in automation rules once the space has rhythm.

### Playbook 5 — The Ghostwriting Operation
*Running multiple clients through one system.*

1. Every client has a profile (niches, voice) — the Atlas routes captures to clients by niche match, never by guess.
2. Ideas home gives each client a shelf; a client pill opens their board as a one-column scan of serif hooks.
3. Study swipes *in the client's niche* — the taxonomy filters Discover and the Library to their lane.
4. The Content Calendar is the client dashboard: their color, their chips, their scoreboard. Client work and personal work run through the same loop without mixing.

## 4. The Operating Rhythm

The system rewards a rhythm, not constant presence:

- **Morning (10 min)** — Command Center: today's tasks, habits, calendar. Glance at the Shelf: what needs a decision?
- **All day (0 min)** — ⌥C / Telegram / iOS. Capture and forget. *Never* interrupt work to organize.
- **Once daily (10–15 min)** — Inbox to zero, from the keyboard.
- **3–4× weekly (45–90 min)** — a **deep block**: either a Study session (Playbook 2) or a bench session (Playbook 1, steps 2–5). This is the block that actually compounds; protect it.
- **Weekly (30 min)** — the review: weekly report, calendar scoreboard, ripe seedlings → Concept Desk, prune the Shelf, one `?` self-test on the topic you studied.

## 5. Power Moves

1. **`?` before you search the web.** Ask Cortex first — you've probably already captured the answer.
2. **`@kind` scoping in ⌘K** narrows Ask/search to one atom type.
3. **Drag results out of ⌘K** straight onto the canvas — retrieval as placement.
4. **Space peeks almost everything** — swipe cards, library items, thinkspace files. Stop opening things to look at them.
5. **⌥C accepts drags** — screenshots, images from Safari, files from Mail. It's not just a text box.
6. **Captures can advance questions.** When triaging, the "advance question" route is the highest-value verb in the Inbox — use it deliberately.
7. **Pin a seedling** to force-ripen it when you know it's ready before the numbers do.
8. **Link swipes AND concepts to an idea** before writing. Swipes give structure, concepts give substance; the draft writes itself.
9. **Name clusters like charters** ("Objection-handling angles for X", not "Misc") — the router literally reads them.
10. **Slash commands in capture** are your personal grammar — one keystroke to route to a lane.
11. **Telegram topic aliases** turn phone capture into routed capture.
12. **Panes as tabs**: split the Study next to an Idea bench — read on one side, develop on the other.
13. **The inline assistant is everywhere** — stop leaving the room to "go ask AI." `/riff` in the margin beats a chat window.
14. **Turn on the built-in automations** once you trust the loop — they ship disabled on purpose.
15. **⌘D places + portals** make big thinkspaces navigable — stop scroll-hunting.

## 6. The Adoption Ladder

You don't learn this system at once; you climb it. Each level is useful alone and makes the next one obvious:

- **Level 1 — Capture + Inbox.** ⌥C everything, triage daily. (The system now sees everything you think.)
- **Level 2 — Command Center + Swipe file.** Run your day and stock your references. (The system now runs your ops and your taste.)
- **Level 3 — Study.** Inquiry sessions with debriefs; Swipe Study with Use-in-Idea. (The system now compounds understanding.)
- **Level 4 — Crystallize + Create.** Concept Desk, concept pages, Idea bench → Content pipeline → Calendar. (The system now converts understanding into output.)
- **Level 5 — Close the loop.** Scoreboard, patterns, automations, `?` self-tests. (The system now improves itself.)

Most tools stop at Level 2. The entire point of Cosmo is that Levels 3–5 exist and feed each other: **you study to grow concepts, you grow concepts to load ideas, you load ideas so writing is easy, you ship so the scoreboard teaches you what to study next.** That's the loop. Everything else is furniture.
